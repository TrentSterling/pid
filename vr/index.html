TWO INDEPENDENT CHOICES

Hand control and player movement have different jobs.

  1. Choose a grab controller

    Hold the same cube and compare how it follows and reacts on the player.

  2. Choose player contact

    Cast correction moves the origin directly. The force servo has a finite response. Off isolates grabbing.

  3. Replay the experiment

    Check airborne motion, one/two-hand floor pushes, wall pressure, and an external shove.

What am I actually comparing?

The browser uses Cannon-es rigidbodies. Its point joint constrains a grab point and leaves rotation free; it is not Unity’s ConfigurableJoint. The custom modes apply bounded forces or impulses and torques. The velocity servo targets a one-step velocity with a finite impulse budget. The Unity starter uses a real ConfigurableJoint, with its own solver and tuning.

Choose cast correction, finite-force contact servo, or no hand locomotion independently. Hands here are massless tracking/contact proxies, not fully simulated hand rigidbodies. Static sweeps use expanded boxes. Our velocity mode is a teaching experiment, not FireBlast’s unpublished implementation. The original GorillaLocomotion C# is included unchanged for reference.

Headset controls

Grip near a cube or cyan fixed grip. Trigger the room’s mode buttons to switch without releasing. The room’s Contact mode button cycles cast / force / off independently. On controllers exposing standard face buttons: A cycles modes, X resets. Pull on floor or wall contacts to move.

Physical turning and room-scale movement work directly. This teaching room has no artificial turning or joystick locomotion in VR. Desktop uses WASD and adjustable hand poses.
