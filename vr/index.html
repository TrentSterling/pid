Shared tuning

Frequency and damping apply to the force controllers. The point joint uses the engine’s constraint solver.

Limits & body mass

Finite force is intentional. A weak drive cannot support the player’s weight. Cube mass updates preserve its velocity; reset for a fresh comparison.

THE EXPERIMENT

Don’t throw out the joint. Put it on a switch.

  1. Pick up the same cube

    Use the guided workout, desktop hand controls, or your headset’s grip buttons.

  2. Switch while it’s held

    Compare lag, angular wobble, and how much the object moves the player.

  3. Test against the world

    Push a cube into the wall. Grab a fixed cyan grip and pull. Watch the force cap.

What am I actually comparing?

The browser uses Cannon-es rigidbodies. Its point joint constrains a grab point and leaves rotation free; it is not Unity’s ConfigurableJoint. The other three modes apply bounded forces and torques. The Unity starter uses a real ConfigurableJoint, with its own solver and tuning.

The arm locomotion is an educational adaptation of the original GorillaLocomotion approach, using swept hand contacts against static boxes. The unmodified upstream C# is included for reference.

Headset controls

Grip near a cube or cyan fixed grip. Trigger the room’s mode buttons to switch without releasing. On controllers exposing standard face buttons: A cycles modes, X resets. Pull on floor or wall contacts to move.

Physical turning and room-scale movement work directly. This teaching room has no artificial turning or joystick locomotion in VR. Desktop uses WASD and adjustable hand poses.
