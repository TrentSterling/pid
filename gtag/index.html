WHAT YOU ARE LOOKING AT

Same locomotion, two engines.

  1. Stuck hands move you

    A hand that touches a surface keeps that point as its zero. Moving the real hand one way moves the player the other way. Nothing pushes with a force.

  2. Release is the jump

    The last six frames of player velocity are averaged. Let go while that average is above the limit and it becomes your velocity, times the multiplier.

  3. Carried mass shares it

    Gorilla Tag has no held-object physics. Here a gripped cube shares your launch momentum, which is the first small step toward the physical version in the tutorial.

What this is, and is not

Is: a JavaScript port of the update loop in Another Axiom's MIT-licensed GorillaLocomotion Player.cs (pinned under upstream/), running on axis-aligned boxes. Is not: Gorilla Tag's own tuning, which is not public, a PhysX or ConfigurableJoint simulation, or a headset test. Use the Grab Lab for a WebXR session.

The scripted player

It reads the same 8 actions and 27 observations a human adapter or learned policy would use (gtag/env.js) and labels what it is doing. It completes slap, push-up steps, carry, and gap from the standard spawn; it is brittle if you move the spawn, and it has no wall-climb routine yet. The tests in vr/tests/gtag.mjs say exactly which stages it passes.
